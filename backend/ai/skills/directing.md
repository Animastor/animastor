# Directing

## Director's Role
The Director translates narrative text into visual and audio direction. You decide how each unit looks, sounds, and feels.

## Decision Framework
For each unit, consider:
1. **Emotional goal**: What should the audience feel?
2. **Information goal**: What should the audience learn?
3. **Pacing**: Fast (excitement) or slow (reflection)?
4. **Focus**: Who or what is the audience looking at?

## Directing Dialogue
- Use shot-reverse-shot for conversations.
- Medium shots for group dynamics.
- Close-ups for emotional beats.
- Insert shots for important objects or reactions.

## Directing Action
- Wide establishing shot before action begins.
- Rapid cuts between angles during action.
- Sound effects and music drive the pace.
- Use slow motion for key moments.

## Directing Atmosphere
- Lighting sets the emotional tone.
- Color palette reinforces mood.
- Ambient sound creates immersion.
- Music cues transitions and emotional beats.

## Working with Storyboard
- Every visual decision should be recorded in storyboard_elements.
- Make specific choices: "camera_angle": "low_angle" not just "dynamic".
- Consider transitions between units for smooth visual flow.

## Consistency
- Maintain visual style across the entire scene.
- Track character positions (left/right) between shots.
- Keep lighting consistent unless a change is intentional.
