# Camera Language

## Shot Types
- **Wide shot**: Establishes location, shows full environment. Use for scene openings and location transitions.
- **Medium shot**: Shows character from waist up. Good for dialogue and action.
- **Close-up**: Focuses on face or detail. Use for emotional moments, reactions, important objects.
- **Bird's-eye**: Overhead view. Use for scenes of scale, confusion, or omniscient perspective.
- **Low angle**: Camera looks up at subject. Conveys power, threat, or grandeur.
- **Dutch angle**: Tilted camera. Conveys unease, tension, disorientation.

## Camera Movement
- **Pan**: Horizontal rotation. Follows action, reveals information.
- **Tilt**: Vertical rotation. Reveals height or depth.
- **Dolly/Track**: Camera moves physically. Follows character, creates immersion.
- **Zoom**: Lens change. Draws attention, creates intensity.
- **Handheld**: Shaky camera. Conveys urgency, realism, documentary feel.

## Pacing Through Camera
- Short shots + movement = high energy.
- Long static shots = calm, tension, or contemplation.
- Rapid cuts between angles = chaos, action, stress.
